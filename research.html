Research

Making unstructured text legible to systems

My research sits at the intersection of NLP and applied machine learning — asking how language, once embedded and structured, can drive genuinely useful decisions.

My graduate research centered on scoring semantic similarity between augmented sentence pairs, using autoencoders and a Siamese BERT architecture — work that pushed me to think carefully about how meaning is represented, compared, and evaluated in embedding space.

Since then I've carried that language-first lens into applied roles: building knowledge graphs for social analytics, fine-tuning LLMs with LoRA and PEFT, and designing AI agents and RAG pipelines that make retrieval and reasoning traceable rather than opaque.

I'm drawn to problems where language, structure, and time intersect — from semantic scoring to graph-based reasoning to forecasting on sequential data.

Semantic Similarity & Scoring

Autoencoder and Siamese BERT models for scoring augmented sentence pairs on semantic textual similarity.

LLM Fine-Tuning & Agents

Prompt engineering, LoRA/PEFT fine-tuning, RAG pipelines, and AI agent design (via LangChain, MCP).

Graph-Based Analytics

Neo4j graph databases for extracting insight from social and relational data at scale.

Time-Series Forecasting

Predictive modeling with LSTM, XGBoost, ARIMA, and Prophet, applied to traffic-flow prediction.
